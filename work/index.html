PUBLIC · FLAGSHIP

AutoQA Foundation: capability and authority boundaries for LLM-based quality review.

AutoQA Foundation is an independent public framework for deciding what an automated reviewer can legitimately establish, when the record requires abstention, and what must be measured before a finding receives consequential authority.

The project begins with record identifiability: an evaluator cannot recover facts absent from the authorized record or force a unique verdict from an instruction that does not determine one.

It then turns that limit into an operating package:

  • supported, contradicted, insufficient, and contract-ambiguous outcomes;
  • claim-level evidence and provenance;
  • lane-specific calibration and risk bounds;
  • explicit abstention and escalation;
  • named human ownership;
  • a bounded pilot protocol;
  • a decision brief, technical guide, evidence ledger, schemas, and templates.

What it demonstrates: translating a broad concern into a falsifiable operating framework; separating theoretical limits from generation-relative model capability; connecting evaluation evidence to governance and release decisions; stating limitations and non-claims as part of the system.

ESTABLISHED · 2024–PRESENT

Progression from expert data creation to program operations.

I entered frontier-model data work by creating complex mathematics training data. I advanced into reviewing contributors, then auditing reviewer decisions, and later into project development, program management, client collaboration, quality leadership, and broader operations.

This progression gives me a direct view of each interface in the human-data system:

  • how experts interpret a task;
  • how ambiguous guidance produces inconsistent outputs;
  • how reviewers distinguish actual failures from preference;
  • how second-level review exposes calibration defects;
  • how quality signals can be distorted by incentives or tooling;
  • how requirements must evolve without making the program incoherent;
  • how program decisions affect both evidence quality and the people producing it.

Selected work has included managing data and evaluation projects supporting a frontier AI lab through an external AI data partner.

What it demonstrates: judgment under ambiguous specifications; reviewer calibration and adjudication; project and program leadership; translation between clients, operators, experts, reviewers, and technical systems; end-to-end understanding of human-data workflows.

ESTABLISHED · SEVEN PROTOTYPES OVER THREE MONTHS

Developed a pilot evaluation framework through seven prototypes during three months of client collaboration.

The target was not static. The framework had to change as the capability, available evidence, and operational constraints became clearer. The work included:

  • translating broad objectives into observable task requirements;
  • developing calibration benchmarks;
  • designing functionality tests;
  • creating contributor and reviewer guidance;
  • refining review workflows;
  • establishing acceptance criteria;
  • reconciling feedback without allowing the specification to drift into contradiction.

What it demonstrates: iterative evaluation design; disciplined requirement translation; comfort with emerging capabilities and incomplete information; the ability to make ambiguity operational without pretending it has disappeared.

ESTABLISHED · PROGRAMS INVOLVING 3,000+ CONTRIBUTORS

Quality systems for distributed expert work.

Defined technical standards, calibration benchmarks, functionality tests, and reviewer controls for programs involving more than 3,000 contributors.

At that scale, quality cannot depend on a few experienced people informally “knowing what good looks like.” The program needs a shared decision contract, diagnostic feedback, escalation paths, and evidence that disagreements are being resolved consistently.

The work focused on:

  • operational definitions;
  • examples and counterexamples;
  • calibration before consequential review;
  • second-level review of reviewer decisions;
  • functionality and completeness tests;
  • error classification;
  • feedback loops;
  • distinction between contributor error, reviewer error, instruction defect, and missing evidence.

What it demonstrates: program-level quality design; calibration and reviewer governance; systems thinking across a distributed workforce; attention to both operational scale and decision legitimacy.

ESTABLISHED · HUMAN + AI VERIFICATION

Treat model output as a set of claims to test.

Evaluate AI-generated code for correctness, security, and performance, with particular attention to subtle failures that can survive superficial review or automated checks.

Built AI-assisted workflows for claim verification and calibrated quality review. Coordinated multi-agent workflows for planning, code review, and verification.

The underlying method is consistent:

  1. identify the concrete claims in the output;
  2. determine what evidence would support or contradict each claim;
  3. retrieve or generate that evidence through files, tests, tools, or authoritative sources;
  4. preserve provenance;
  5. separate supported findings from unresolved questions;
  6. assign consequential interpretation to an accountable owner.

This work informed AutoQA Foundation's distinction between producing a plausible critique and earning the authority to decide.

What it demonstrates: evidence-oriented review; technical evaluation of AI-generated artifacts; workflow design across human and automated components; attention to false accusations as well as missed defects.

PUBLIC · EXPERIMENTAL WORKING FORK

Agentic Council: adversarial review that does not trust the acting model's self-report.

My public repository is a working fork of Agentic Council, an open-source multi-model adversarial-review engine.

The system asks critics from different model families to review an acting model's claims against an explicit quality bar. During review, critics can request evidence from files, allowlisted web sources, or sandboxed commands. The acting and judging roles are separated, and the critics are not expected to accept the actor's narrative as ground truth.

The project makes several useful design commitments visible: evidence access rather than memory-only critique; read-only critic roles; review before or immediately around state-changing actions; explicit verdicts and audit records; measurement of critic usefulness rather than blind trust in model consensus.

Limitations: a council of models is not automatically accurate because it is diverse or adversarial. Repeated votes do not create missing evidence, and consensus does not confer authority. The project should be treated as an experimental verification architecture whose components require measurement.

IN DEVELOPMENT

Real-data agent and control environments.

I am developing long-horizon agent and control tasks that use real or measured data, hidden variation, explicit baselines, and reproducible grading.

The goal is to convert scarce domain expertise into reusable training and evaluation signal while preserving the structure that makes the domain difficult.

Design priorities include real or measured source data with clear license and provenance; interaction over time rather than one-shot prediction; hidden conditions that prevent memorization of a single scenario; deterministic or reproducible grading; strong reference solutions and weak baselines; transparent resource requirements; tests that distinguish task difficulty from grader instability; and public documentation of what is measured and what remains unmeasured.

Limitations: this is active task-authoring work. It is not yet presented as a validated benchmark suite, a published research result, or evidence of general agent capability.

CURRENT DIRECTION

A trust and translation layer between model teams and data owners.

I am extending my background in requirements, evaluation, quality systems, and program operations into specialized-data sourcing and exchange.

The operating problem is not merely matching a buyer with a provider. It is helping both sides answer:

  • What model objective is the data intended to support?
  • What does the provider actually have?
  • How was it produced?
  • What rights and restrictions apply?
  • What sample would reveal technical utility?
  • What constitutes acceptance?
  • What happens when the pilot fails?
  • How will provenance, versioning, and quality findings survive delivery?

Current contribution model: buyer requirement translation; provider and dataset qualification; provenance and documentation review; pilot and sample-evaluation design; acceptance criteria; separation of technical utility from rights confidence; operating standards for marketplaces and exchanges.

This is a current direction, not a claim that I have already operated a large data marketplace.

CURRENT DIRECTION

Governed, testable organizational knowledge.

I am interested in the layer that determines whether internal documents, databases, and operational records can function as reliable inputs for retrieval, fine-tuning, evaluation, or agent workflows.

The work begins with questions that generic “AI strategy” often skips:

  • Which sources are authoritative?
  • Who may access them?
  • How are conflicts and stale records handled?
  • What provenance must follow an answer?
  • How will retrieval be tested?
  • What output qualities matter for the intended use?
  • When must the system abstain or escalate?
  • Which decisions remain human-owned?
  • How will changes in the underlying data be detected?

The aim is not broad transformation language. It is a measurable data and evaluation layer tied to a specific organizational use.

CLOSING

The common thread is translation under uncertainty.

Across expert data, evaluation systems, quality operations, automated review, data partnerships, and agent environments, the same work recurs: define the actual objective; identify the evidence that can bear on it; make the human and technical workflow executable; separate missing information from poor reasoning; test the system before scaling it; preserve uncertainty where the record does not support a stronger conclusion; and assign authority to an accountable owner.
