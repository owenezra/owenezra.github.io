Flagship Public Work · AutoQA Foundation 0.1.0

Define the boundary before automating the verdict.

I created AutoQA Foundation as a public decision and technical system for determining where LLM-assisted quality review is useful, where the available record cannot support a verdict, and what must be measured before a system may influence consequential decisions. It ships with a decision brief, technical guide, evidence ledger, source library, bounded pilot protocol, and decision tools — and it is an independent working framework, not a certification body or an accuracy claim for any particular system.

Read the case study Open AutoQA Foundation ↗

The master limit

Record identifiability: processing cannot recover facts or resolve interpretations absent from the observable record. The record sets the ceiling for any automated reviewer.

  • Identical records, different correct decisions
  • Capability cannot manufacture missing facts
  • Ambiguous rules do not decide themselves

Honest output states

Supported, contradicted, insufficient, and contract-ambiguous remain distinct rather than being forced into pass or fail.

  • Four explicit result states
  • Abstention by design
  • No hidden subjective judgment

Capability is not authority

Automated consequences require lane-level calibration, measured false-accusation risk, abstention, and named human ownership.

  • Bounded pilot protocol
  • Evidence ledger & source library
  • Decision tools & stop conditions

Operating principles

01

Utility is conditional

A dataset is useful only relative to a model objective, training stage, evaluation design, and acceptance threshold.

02

Rights are not quality

Clear provenance and licensing do not establish technical value; technical value does not establish permission to use the data.

03

Evaluation comes first

Decide how success will be measured before scaling collection, annotation, retrieval, or fine-tuning.

04

Automation needs boundaries

Model-assisted review is useful when it narrows human attention and cites evidence — not when it hides subjective judgment behind a score.

05

Open does not mean free

Open specifications, audit standards, and interoperability can improve markets even when datasets remain paid, private, or access-controlled.

06

Accountability requires inspection

The people responsible for a system need to understand its sources, tests, failure modes, and escalation paths.
