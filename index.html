CAPABILITY · EVIDENCE · AUTHORITY

Define the boundary before automating the verdict.

I created AutoQA Foundation as a public, de-branded decision and technical system for determining where LLM-assisted quality review is useful, where the available record cannot support a verdict, and what must be measured before a system may influence consequential decisions.

The project turns a broad argument about “model-as-judge” reliability into an inspectable operating model: explicit result states, capability limits, authorization rules, measurement requirements, a bounded pilot protocol, and a source-level evidence record.

Read the case study Open AutoQA Foundation ↗
  • Master limitRecord identifiability: processing cannot recover facts or resolve interpretations absent from the observable record.
  • Honest outputsSupported, contradicted, insufficient, and contract-ambiguous remain distinct rather than being forced into pass or fail.
  • Authority boundaryAutomated consequences require lane-level calibration, measured false-accusation risk, abstention, and named human ownership.
  • Operational packageDecision brief, theoretical limits, technical guide, evidence ledger, source library, pilot protocol, and decision-note generator.

OPERATING PRINCIPLES

How I think about AI data.

Data work fails when access, quality, rights, and utility are collapsed into one vague promise. I treat them as separate claims that require separate evidence.

Utility is conditional

A dataset is useful only relative to a model objective, training stage, evaluation design, and acceptance threshold.

Rights are not quality

Clear provenance and licensing do not establish technical value; technical value does not establish permission to use the data.

Evaluation comes first

Teams should decide how success will be measured before scaling collection, annotation, retrieval, or fine-tuning.

Automation needs boundaries

Model-assisted review is useful when it narrows human attention and cites evidence—not when it hides subjective judgment behind a score. See the operating boundary.

Open does not mean free

Open specifications, audit standards, and interoperability can improve markets even when datasets remain paid, private, or access-controlled.

Accountability requires inspection

The people responsible for a system need to understand its sources, tests, failure modes, and escalation paths.
