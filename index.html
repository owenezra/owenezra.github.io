FLAGSHIP PUBLIC WORK · AUTOQA FOUNDATION

An evaluator can be useful without being entitled to decide.

AutoQA Foundation is an independent public framework for defining the capability and authority boundaries of LLM-based quality review.

It begins with a simple constraint: the record sets the ceiling. A model can organize, compare, retrieve, and reason over evidence. It cannot recover facts that are absent from the observable record, make an ambiguous instruction decide itself, or acquire authority merely by sounding confident.

The project turns that boundary into an operating system for review: explicit result states, claim-level evidence, abstention, lane-specific calibration, measured risk, named human ownership, and a bounded pilot protocol.

Open the AutoQA case study Open AutoQA Foundation ↗

Independent public working framework. Not a certification body, formal standard, or claim that a particular automated reviewer is accurate.

  • Record identifiabilityWhen the same observable record can correspond to different correct decisions, no evaluator that sees only that record can be correct in every case. Better processing does not manufacture missing facts.
  • Four honest outcomesSupported, contradicted, insufficient, and contract-ambiguous remain distinct. A binary pass/fail system does not remove uncertainty; it hides it.
  • Authority must be earnedA capable detector is not automatically authorized to impose consequences. Authority depends on the exact configured lane, untouched adjudicated data, measured error bounds, abstention behavior, appeal, audit, and accountable ownership.
  • A decision packageAutoQA Foundation includes a decision brief, capability limits, technical guidance, a source-level evidence ledger, pilot controls, schemas, templates, and decision tools.

CURRENT DIRECTION

Make specialized data legible, testable, and transactable.

A data partnership is not successful merely because a dataset changes hands. I am extending my evaluation and program-operations background into this layer.

A model team must be able to explain what capability, failure mode, or evaluation need the data is meant to address. A data owner must be able to explain what exists, how it was produced, what rights travel with it, and what cannot be promised. Both sides need a method for determining whether the data is technically useful, adequately documented, appropriately governed, and maintainable after delivery.

Requirement translation

Convert a broad request into target use, unit of data, population, coverage, representation, format, volume, freshness, constraints, and acceptance evidence.

Provider and dataset qualification

Determine whether the provider can establish provenance, repeatability, documentation quality, delivery capacity, and the technical characteristics needed for a credible pilot.

Utility and rights as separate claims

A dataset may be legally usable but technically weak, or technically valuable but unavailable for the intended use. Neither claim substitutes for the other.

Sample and pilot design

Create a bounded test that lets the buyer evaluate utility before scaling acquisition and lets the provider understand what acceptance actually requires.

Acceptance and delivery

Define measurable acceptance criteria, review procedures, remediation paths, documentation, versioning, and handoff requirements before the transaction becomes difficult to unwind.

Ongoing governance

Preserve provenance, changes, restrictions, quality findings, and evaluation results so the data remains understandable after the original transaction.

ENTERPRISE DATA LAYER

Internal data is not an AI system until it is governed and tested.

Giving a model access to documents, databases, or operational records does not establish that the resulting system is grounded, reliable, or fit for use.

The difficult layer is deciding which sources are authoritative, who may access them, how conflicts are resolved, what retrieval should return, how outputs are evaluated, what traceability is required, when a human must intervene, and which decisions the system is not allowed to make.

This is not generic “AI transformation.” It is the concrete data and evaluation layer that determines whether an implementation can be trusted for its stated purpose.

The same operating discipline used in training-data and evaluation programs applies here:

  • define the intended use;
  • inventory authoritative and non-authoritative sources;
  • map permissions and restrictions;
  • identify missing or conflicting knowledge;
  • create retrieval and output evaluations;
  • preserve citations and provenance;
  • specify escalation and abstention;
  • monitor drift and source changes;
  • separate experimental usefulness from production authority.

PRINCIPLES

How I think about AI data and evaluation.

Data work fails when access, quality, rights, and utility are collapsed into one vague promise. I treat them as separate claims that require separate evidence.

The record sets the ceiling

A system cannot establish facts that are absent from its authorized evidence. More tokens, agents, votes, or confidence do not change what is observable.

Utility is conditional

A dataset is useful only relative to a model objective, training stage, evaluation design, and acceptance threshold.

Rights are not quality

Clear provenance and licensing do not establish technical value. Technical value does not establish permission to use the data.

Evaluation comes before scale

Teams should decide how success will be measured before expanding collection, annotation, retrieval, fine-tuning, or acquisition.

Automation needs abstention

A useful reviewer must be able to say supported, contradicted, insufficient, or contract-ambiguous—and route each state appropriately.

Human disagreement is diagnostic

Variance can indicate expertise gaps, attention failures, ambiguous rules, missing evidence, or genuinely plural judgment. The system should diagnose the source rather than average it away.

Accountability requires inspection

People responsible for a system need access to its sources, tests, failure modes, changes, escalation paths, and decision owners.

Open infrastructure can support private data

Open specifications, schemas, audit methods, and interoperability can improve a market even when the underlying datasets remain paid, private, or access controlled.
